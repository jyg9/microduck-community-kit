Layout. The control bar talks to the local Python server over GET /api/ports and the WebSocket at /ws. Everything runs offline; the 3D view is a hand-written WebGL1 renderer and the plots are 2D canvas.

3D attitude. The board is 60x40x4 units with the top face coloured and a connector nub; the axes are board-frame X (red), Y (green), Z (blue). A fixed camera with a ground grid and a faint wire sphere shows orientation without ambiguity. The rotation comes from the quat field (trunk attitude, w,x,y,z, already mount-corrected) composed with the fixed mounting rotation BOARD_MOUNT (chip +Z = trunk +X), so what you see is the board in its real mounted attitude: a correctly mounted board stands vertical (PCB plane = trunk Y-Z), it is not drawn flat. An identity orientation means the trunk is level, which for this mounting puts the board upright on its edge with chip +X pointing down. The gravity arrow and the trace stay in the trunk frame, so they are not double-rotated.

Gravity and up. The gravity sample field is the gravity direction expressed in the trunk frame. The 3D view rotates it into the world frame, where it must point along world -Z; the orange arrow is that measured gravity and the cyan arrow is the fixed world up (+Z) reference. "grav dev" is the angle between them in degrees - it stays near 0 when the attitude is consistent with the accelerometer and grows if the fusion drifts or the mounting correction is wrong.

Orientation trace. The faint line is the trail of where the trunk +Z axis has pointed on the unit sphere (about 9 s of history). A tight dot means a stable board; a growing band or scatter means jitter, vibration or yaw drift.

Board settings. The simulation source is selected by sim mode (static, sine, drift, noise, spin, sflp-wait, frozen); amplitude and frequency are per-mille values (1000 = 1.000 in the board's own units), the gyro bias is in raw counts, frame selects whether the node reports the chip or the trunk frame and debug is the firmware log level. "Apply" writes the current form, "Save to flash" writes and then commands a flash save, and the remaining buttons send the factory-defaults, reboot and restart-model commands.

Frames. gyro is the raw chip gyro in deg/s, gyro_trunk_dps is the same vector rotated into the trunk frame by the mount correction, and gravity is also in the trunk frame. The board "frame" setting selects whether the node reports the chip frame or the trunk frame; the host always receives both gyro variants so the plots stay comparable.

Scrolling plots. Two stacked time series (attitude roll/pitch/yaw in deg and gyro x/y/z in deg/s) scroll right to left over a 6 s window, drawn on requestAnimationFrame. The y axis autoscales (it expands instantly and relaxes slowly); "lock Y" freezes it to the entered min/max so offsets are comparable between runs. A gap in a trace means samples were missing over that interval, and the bright vertical line at the right edge is the moving time cursor (now).

Density waterfall. The heat map is drawn pixel by pixel on a canvas: x is time (40 columns per second, right edge = now, about 13 s of history), y is the value of the selected quantity binned into rows, and colour intensity is the number of samples that landed in a bin during that time column (normalised per column, square-root boosted, so the shape of the distribution stays visible). It makes gyro noise floors, quantisation steps and slow offsets visible as bands that march up or down - the classic jitter/drift view. Out-of-range values are clamped to the edge bins and the range slowly adapts to the data.

Statistics. Per axis for gyro (deg/s) and gravity (unit vector): mean, standard deviation, peak-to-peak, least-squares drift slope (units per second, fitted on the buffered window) and min/max. Drift is the slope of a straight-line fit v(t) = a + b*t, so b is reported per second; a persistent non-zero slope is an offset/thermal drift indicator. Link numbers come from the server: achieved poll rate, median/p95/min/max round-trip latency, timeout count, bad frames (CRC/parse errors) and extra bytes on the bus.

Counters. "missing samples" counts gaps in the board sample counter (counter continuity), "stale run" is the current number of consecutive polls where the board returned the identical telemetry block - stale means no new conversion, so a long run points at a frozen sensor, a wrong read rate or a cached register - and "stale max" is the worst run seen since the last connect. Raw flags are decoded to names such as sflp_valid, gyro_sat, sensor_err, simulated or frozen.

Recording. "Record CSV" buffers every received sample in the browser; pressing it again stops and downloads a CSV with time, euler, quaternion (corrected and raw), both gyro frames, gravity, accel, counters, flags and latency.
